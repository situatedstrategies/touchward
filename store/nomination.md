## Description (why this app, in one screen)

Touchward turns "I did the thing" into a physical moment. You finish a task,
open the app, tap a glowing button, and get a haptic pattern and a ripple of
light back. That is the entire loop, on purpose. It is a tiny ritual for
closing loops, for people who run on small wins, and for anyone whose day
needs a little more dopamine and a little less friction.

It is built as a craft object. Every shape, from the icon's wavy ring to a
heart or a star, glows with a pale edge and a bright rim, and the hold timer
traces the button's own outline as you press. Each tap sends a ripple out in
that same outline. Seven reward modes pair a motion with a haptic, from a
single soft pulse to cascading waves. Colors are chosen on a real color
wheel with hex entry and saved into per setting libraries. One press of
Random invents a whole new look, and Save keeps the ones worth keeping.

Haptics are the heart of it. On iPhone the app plays composed patterns
through Core Haptics with real amplitude and sharpness control, offers five
strengths from Gentle to Max, and opens a library of over 150 haptic presets
so a tap can feel like a bell, a heartbeat, a purr, or an anvil.

Touchward respects attention. There is no account, no feed, no streaks to
maintain, no ads, and nothing to sign in to. Reminders are local. Calendar
nudges fire when a meeting ends, on the phone, from the calendar you already
keep, without any of it leaving the device. It is a one time purchase.

## What makes it notable on Apple platforms

- Core Haptics, used with intent: composed patterns with amplitude and
  sharpness envelopes, five strength levels that stack transients and rumble
  past the motor's ceiling, and 150 plus presets. The Feel tab plays each
  choice as you pick it.
- Apple Watch companion: the same button and ripples on the wrist, the
  phone's reminders mirrored as watch notifications, taps counted across both
  devices, synced over WatchConnectivity with no account.
- Action Button, Back Tap, and Shortcuts: the touchward://reward link fires a
  tap from anywhere, so the reward can be one physical gesture.
- Time Sensitive notifications, opt in, so a nudge can reach someone inside
  a Focus mode they set up for deep work.
- EventKit calendar nudges: a notification when each event ends, scheduled
  locally and refreshed by a background task, with event data never stored.
- SwiftUI on watchOS and a React Native app on iPhone sharing one design
  system: the same sampled outlines drive the button, the ripples, and the
  timer on both platforms.
- Privacy manifest with a single declared item (crash data, not linked, not
  for tracking), no third party analytics, and the full source public on
  GitHub so every claim can be checked.
- iPad: the same app laid out larger, not a stretched phone screen.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Every control carries a label and role for VoiceOver; the color picker
accepts typed hex codes for people who cannot use a wheel; haptic strength
runs from Gentle for sensory sensitivity to Max for people who need more
feedback; text follows the system theme or a chosen backdrop with contrast
adjusted automatically; the whole app works with no account and no reading
beyond a few short hints. The app was designed with ADHD and executive
function in mind: one action, an immediate physical reward, and reminders
that attach to the calendar rather than to yet another list.

## The story behind it

Touchward comes from Situated Strategies, a small independent studio. The
first version was called Dopamine and lived on one phone; the wavy blue rings
of the icon became the whole visual language. It grew into a product because
the people who tried it kept asking for the button back.
